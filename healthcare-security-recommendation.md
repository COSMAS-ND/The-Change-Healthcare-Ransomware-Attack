# Healthcare Security Recommendations

## Introduction

The Change Healthcare ransomware attack demonstrates the need for healthcare organizations to strengthen both cybersecurity defenses and operational resilience.

The following recommendations focus on reducing the likelihood and impact of similar incidents.

## 1. Identity and Access Management

Healthcare organizations should enforce strong identity and access controls across critical systems.

Recommended controls:

- Use unique accounts for all users.
- Enforce least privilege.
- Review privileged accounts regularly.
- Remove inactive accounts promptly.
- Monitor privileged activity.
- Apply strong authentication to remote and administrative access.

## 2. Multi-Factor Authentication

Multi-factor authentication should be mandatory for critical systems, remote access, privileged accounts, and administrative interfaces.

MFA provides an additional layer of protection when passwords or credentials are compromised.

## 3. Network Segmentation

Critical healthcare systems should be segmented so that compromise of one environment does not automatically provide access to other critical systems.

Segmentation should be combined with access controls, monitoring, and regular validation.

## 4. Backup and Recovery

Organizations should maintain reliable backups of critical systems and data.

Backups should be:

- Regularly tested
- Protected from unauthorized modification
- Appropriately isolated
- Available for rapid recovery

Recovery exercises should be conducted regularly so that organizations understand how long critical services would take to restore.

## 5. Incident Response

Healthcare organizations should maintain a documented incident-response plan covering:

- Detection
- Analysis
- Containment
- Eradication
- Recovery
- Communication
- Post-incident review

The plan should be tested through simulations and exercises.

## 6. Security Monitoring

Organizations should continuously monitor critical systems for suspicious activity.

Security teams should establish monitoring and alerting for:

- Unusual authentication activity
- Privileged-account abuse
- Suspicious remote access
- Abnormal network traffic
- Malware indicators
- Unexpected system changes

## 7. Third-Party Risk Management

Healthcare organizations should identify critical vendors and technology dependencies.

Vendor assessments should consider:

- Security controls
- Incident-response capabilities
- Business continuity
- Recovery capabilities
- Data protection
- Notification procedures

Organizations should also maintain contingency plans for the failure or unavailability of critical third-party services.

## 8. Business Continuity

Critical healthcare processes should have alternative procedures when primary systems are unavailable.

Organizations should identify essential services and determine:

- Maximum tolerable downtime
- Alternative workflows
- Communication procedures
- Emergency escalation processes
- Recovery priorities

Business-continuity plans should be tested regularly.

## 9. Security Governance

Cybersecurity should be treated as an enterprise-level risk.

Leadership should:

- Establish clear cybersecurity accountability.
- Review critical cyber risks regularly.
- Ensure appropriate security resources.
- Track remediation of significant vulnerabilities.
- Conduct regular security assessments.
- Ensure cybersecurity is included in business-continuity planning.

## 10. Recommended Priority Actions

### Priority 1 — Critical

- Implement MFA for critical and remote access.
- Review privileged accounts.
- Validate backups and recovery procedures.
- Identify critical systems and dependencies.

### Priority 2 — High

- Strengthen network segmentation.
- Improve security monitoring.
- Test incident-response procedures.
- Assess critical third-party providers.

### Priority 3 — Ongoing

- Conduct regular security assessments.
- Train personnel.
- Review access privileges.
- Conduct business-continuity exercises.
- Update incident-response plans.

## Conclusion

Healthcare cybersecurity must protect more than information.

It must protect the availability of healthcare services, continuity of care, financial operations, patient privacy, and organizational resilience.

The Change Healthcare incident demonstrates why prevention, detection, response, recovery, and governance must work together as one security strategy.
