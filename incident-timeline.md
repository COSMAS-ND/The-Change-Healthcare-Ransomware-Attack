# WannaCry Incident Timeline

## Case File 001

### Overview

This timeline reconstructs the major events associated with the WannaCry ransomware outbreak of 2017, with emphasis on vulnerability exploitation, rapid network propagation, and the impact on healthcare organizations.

---

## Timeline

### March 2017 — Vulnerability Disclosure

Microsoft released security updates addressing critical vulnerabilities affecting the Server Message Block (SMB) protocol.

### April 2017 — Exploit Becomes Public

The vulnerability became associated with publicly available exploitation capabilities, increasing the risk to unpatched systems.

### May 12, 2017 — Major WannaCry Outbreak

WannaCry began spreading rapidly across vulnerable Windows systems worldwide.

The malware combined ransomware behavior with network-propagation capabilities, allowing infected systems to become a source of further infections.

### May 12–13, 2017 — Rapid Global Spread

Organizations across multiple sectors were affected. Healthcare organizations experienced significant operational disruption, particularly where vulnerable and unpatched systems remained in use.

### May 13, 2017 — Defensive Response Accelerates

Organizations began implementing emergency mitigation measures, including security updates, network isolation, system recovery, and increased monitoring.

### Following the Outbreak — Remediation

Security teams focused on patching vulnerable systems, improving endpoint protection, strengthening network segmentation, and reviewing incident-response procedures.

---

## Key Investigation Observations

- Unpatched systems represented a major risk factor.
- The ability to propagate across networks increased the speed and scale of the incident.
- Healthcare environments faced significant operational consequences.
- Patch management and network segmentation were important defensive controls.
- Incident response required coordination between technical and operational teams.

---

## Analyst Note

The WannaCry incident demonstrates how a known vulnerability can become a major organizational risk when vulnerable systems remain exposed and defensive controls are insufficient.
